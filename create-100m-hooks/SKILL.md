# Create 100M Hooks

## Workflow

Begin with a focused grill-me interview before writing hooks. Ask one question at a time, include a recommended answer, and continue until the minimum brief is complete.

Minimum brief:

- Platform and placement: X/Twitter post, thread, QRT, reply, TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts, YouTube title, LinkedIn, email, landing page, ad, cold DM, etc.
- Niche/use case and offer: what is being sold or promoted.
- Target audience: who should instantly think "this is for me."
- Features, mechanism, or proof: what makes the offer believable or different.
- Pain, desire, and promise: what the audience wants, fears, or wants to avoid.
- Tone and boundaries: contrarian, premium, direct-response, founder-led, educational, compliant, no profanity, regulated claims, etc.
- Output size: default to 15 hooks when the user does not specify; produce 10-20 when requested.

If the user already provided enough information, skip redundant questions and generate. If an answer is vague, ask the next highest-impact question instead of asking for everything at once.

Load `references/hook-playbook-patterns.md` before generating hooks. Also load `references/twitter-orca-style.md` when the user asks for X/Twitter, viral devtool content, Orca content, AI coding agents, or similar founder/build-in-public posts.

## Interview Style

Use the `grill-me` pattern:

- Ask one question at a time.
- Give your recommended answer below each question.
- Resolve dependencies before moving on. For example, do not ask for tone before you understand audience awareness and platform.
- If the user asks to "just make assumptions," state the assumptions briefly and proceed.

Recommended first question:

`What platform and placement are these hooks for, and what are you trying to sell or get people to do? If this is for Orca on X/Twitter, what should the post make people feel: curiosity, FOMO, trust, or urgency to try it?`

Recommended answer format:

`Recommended: "X/Twitter posts for developers and AI founders, selling Orca as infra for parallel AI coding sessions; goal is followers, waitlist signups, and repo stars."`

## Generation Rules

Create hooks that combine:

1. A callout that selects the right audience.
2. A value implication that makes attention feel worth the cost.
3. A specific tension: pain, desire, status, speed, money, risk, proof, contrast, curiosity, or identity.
4. A format family from the reference: label, question, conditional, command, statement, list/steps, story, provocative line, or nonverbal/visual setup.

Make the batch varied:

- About 70% should remix proven/direct formats that are likely to work immediately.
- About 20% should be sharper permutations: different angle, audience segment, proof point, or visual wrapper.
- About 10% should be riskier outliers: more contrarian, surprising, or story-led.

Avoid generic hooks such as "Unlock your potential" or "Here are tips for..." unless the audience and payoff are made concrete.

## Output

Default output:

- A clean numbered list of hooks.
- Include a short parenthetical tag only when useful, such as `(question)`, `(contrarian)`, `(visual)`, `(email)`, or `(ad)`.
- Keep each hook tight enough for the requested platform. For X/Twitter, prefer lowercase, short lines, one idea per line, and no corporate phrasing unless the user asks otherwise.

When the user asks for content material, add one compact expansion per selected hook:

- Video/ad: opening shot, first spoken line, beat outline, CTA.
- Email: subject line, preview text, first 3-5 lines.
- X/Twitter: hook, post body, optional 1/ 2/ 3/ thread beats, QRT/reply variant, and CTA.
- Social post: hook, body bullets, CTA.
- Outreach: opener, relevance line, ask.
- Landing page: headline, subheadline, proof/supporting bullets.

When the user asks for ranking, score hooks on:

- Audience callout clarity.
- Value promise strength.
- Specificity.
- Curiosity gap.
- Believability/proof.
- Platform fit.

Do not claim guaranteed performance or promise virality. Frame recommendations as likely stronger candidates to test, and suggest posting variants when useful.
