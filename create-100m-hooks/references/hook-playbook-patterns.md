# Hook Playbook Patterns

This reference distills the local $100M Hooks-style playbook into reusable principles and original working formats. Do not reproduce the source playbook's example bank verbatim; use these patterns to create fresh hooks for the user's offer.

## Core Model

A strong hook sells attention. It makes the right viewer feel selected, then implies that staying will be worth it.

Use this structure:

`[callout / attention signal] + [condition or curiosity] + [value implication]`

Examples of the parts:

- Callout: audience label, identity, situation, platform behavior, pain, result, stage of journey.
- Condition: if/when/before/after, mistake, comparison, hidden rule, contrarian claim, deadline, threshold.
- Value implication: save time, make money, avoid loss, increase status, reduce pain, get clarity, gain control, feel understood.

## Verbal Hook Families

Use these as format families, not rigid templates.

### Labels

Call out the audience by identity or situation.

- `For [specific audience] who [specific struggle/desire]...`
- `[Audience], this is why [desired outcome] feels harder than it should.`
- `If you are a [role] with [problem], steal this.`

### Questions

Create a high-stakes yes/no or open-loop question.

- `Would you rather [bad status quo] or [specific better outcome] by [timeframe/mechanism]?`
- `What if [common belief] is the reason [audience] cannot [goal]?`
- `Why do [audience] keep [painful behavior] even after [effort]?`

### Conditionals

Set a condition that selects qualified attention.

- `If [audience] wants [outcome] without [hated tradeoff], start here.`
- `If [pain] keeps happening, your problem is probably [unexpected cause].`
- `Before you [common action], fix [hidden bottleneck].`

### Commands

Tell the audience what to do when the payoff is clear.

- `Stop [common behavior] until you understand [missing principle].`
- `Watch this before you [costly decision].`
- `Steal this [asset/process] if you want [result].`

### Statements

Make a concise claim that feels useful, surprising, or status-relevant.

- `The fastest way to [outcome] is not [common tactic]. It is [mechanism].`
- `Most [audience] do not have a [surface problem]. They have a [real problem].`
- `The top [small percent] of [audience] all do this differently.`

### Lists Or Steps

Promise a concrete set of items or a ranked path.

- `[Number] ways to [outcome] without [painful tradeoff].`
- `The [number]-step fix for [problem] that [audience] usually overcomplicate.`
- `I ranked [number] [tools/tactics/mistakes] for [audience].`

### Story Or Cold Open

Start at a moment of tension, then connect it to the lesson.

- `I found out [painful/surprising event] because [specific moment].`
- `A [customer/person] came to us with [problem]. [Unexpected result] happened after [mechanism].`
- `The first time I tried [thing], I missed [hidden truth].`

### Provocative Or Contrarian

Challenge a common belief without becoming vague rage-bait.

- `[Common advice] is keeping [audience] stuck.`
- `You do not need more [obvious solution]. You need [specific missing mechanism].`
- `The uncomfortable reason [audience] are not getting [result].`

## Nonverbal And Platform Hooks

For video or visual platforms, pair the words with an immediate attention signal:

- Visual proof: dashboard, before/after, teardown, handwritten note, messy workflow, customer artifact.
- Pattern break: unusual camera angle, silent first frame with bold text, direct eye contact, object in hand, sudden cut to result.
- Sound or pacing: interruptive first beat, pause before the claim, quick comparison, fast teardown.
- On-screen text: make it more specific than the spoken line; use it as the callout or the contradiction.

## 70/20/10 Batch Method

When generating a batch:

- 70% core: direct hooks based on obvious pains, desires, and proven formats.
- 20% permutations: change the audience segment, mechanism, proof, timeframe, emotion, or visual wrapper.
- 10% outliers: bolder contrarian, story-led, or highly specific hooks worth testing.

For 15 hooks, aim for about 10 core, 3 permutations, and 2 outliers.

## Quality Checklist

Before finalizing, revise any hook that fails these checks:

- Does it identify who should care?
- Does it imply clear value if they keep reading/watching?
- Is it specific enough to feel real?
- Is there a curiosity gap without clickbait emptiness?
- Does it fit the platform's attention window?
- Is the claim believable from the user's proof, feature set, or mechanism?
- Would the target audience feel seen rather than broadly marketed to?

## Useful Input Prompts

Use these one at a time when information is missing:

- `Who exactly should hear this and immediately think "this is for me"?`
- `What painful status quo are they stuck in right now?`
- `What specific result does your product create, and how fast or reliably?`
- `What feature, mechanism, proof, or customer story makes the result believable?`
- `What are they already trying that is not working?`
- `What claim would your competitors be afraid to make but you can defend?`
- `What platform constraint matters most: character count, first 2 seconds, subject line length, compliance, or brand tone?`
